序 Preface: no calendar

树无历

单文件，保存后断网也能开

A tree keeps no calendar. What we call a season is bookkeeping: a running sum of warmth, a count of cold nights, a soil bucket draining. In this notebook fifty trees of Asia run on one small climate engine, and each tree’s calendar is predicted from a temperature and rainfall normal alone. Where the prediction fits, the season was never a date. Where it breaks, it is reported in place, marked in red.

thesisDeciduousness is not one thing. In temperate East Asia, cold and daylength write the calendar. In monsoon Asia, water does. Camphor and bamboo swap their leaves in spring without ever standing bare. Palms and mangroves run no calendar at all. So the rows below are not sorted by latitude but by what keeps the clock.
Each ribbon is one year, January to December, at the species’ home station. Green is leaf on the crown, amber is senescence, a dot is flower, a line is fruit. Click a name to open the specimen.

03

叶账 The leaf ledger

A leaf is a loan. It costs carbon to build and pays it back by the day. A deciduous leaf is cheap and fast but works only through a season; an evergreen leaf is dear and slow but keeps working, for one year or for many. The ledger asks which loan a site’s climate will honour.

04

花锋 The bloom front

The cherry-blossom front is the most watched phenology on earth, and it is a thermal integral: a cold bank that must be paid before buds will listen, then a warmth sum that must be paid before they open. Move the slider and the front moves; push it far enough and the chill account fails.

年轮 The ring, and who can read it

“One ring, one year” is a rule of thumb for places that have a winter or a dry season. This station reads a cross-section of any tree at any station, then hands you a core with a ring missing and asks you to find the gap.

06

大年 Mast: the long fast

Some forests fruit in bursts, years apart, all at once. The standard reading is predator satiation: starve the seed-eaters for years, then feed them more than they can eat. The model below lets you starve, feed and bring in mobile predators, and then asks what the best waiting time really is.

07

垂直带 The vertical transect

Climb a Himalayan flank and the belts of Asia stack in a few kilometres: sal and teak at the foot, oak and rhododendron in the mid-slope, fir and birch at the top, then a line where trees stop. Temperature falls 6.5 °C per kilometre; the treeline follows a growing-season isotherm.
